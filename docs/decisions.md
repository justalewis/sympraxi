# Technical decisions

Short records of choices made while building, with the reason, so later contributors don't have to guess. Newest first.

## Phase 1: attendance, blocks and reminders (October 2026)

**Attendance belongs to each student, not to the appointment.** In a group, some students come and some don't, so attendance is kept on `Participant`: came, no-show, or not marked, with who marked it and when. The appointment keeps its two statuses ("Two statuses and three flags", below). A no-show still held the tutor's time, so its appointment stays scheduled and the overlap constraint needs no change. A late cancellation is an ordinary cancellation that frees the time, with `late_cancel` set on the student's row. Adding "completed" or "missed" statuses to the appointment would have had to say something about the whole group, and would have mixed what happened to the time with what happened to each student.

**A final session record means the student came.** Finalizing a record marks its student as here, and a student with a final record can't be marked a no-show. A record finalized after a no-show was confirmed overrides it, since the record shows the session happened. Records finalized before this slice were marked by records migration 0003.

**Possible no-shows are flagged, never decided.** Each schedule sets a grace period, 15 minutes by default. Each minute the scheduler flags any student no one has marked by then. A flag only asks staff to check: they confirm the no-show, mark the student as here, or clear the flag, and nothing counts until they do. Tentative appointments, placeholders, and students with any session record, even a draft, aren't flagged. The sweep looks back two days, so an upgrade doesn't flag a term of unmarked history; staff can still mark older appointments by hand. Tutors mark appointments they teach; front desk staff and directors mark any. A confirmed no-show sends the student a short email that says the session was recorded as missed, gives the count for the term, says what happens at the center's limit, and invites them to reply if they did come or something kept them away.

**Late cancellations are off until a schedule turns them on.** Each schedule sets a window, for example 120 minutes; a cancellation inside it is late. The window is separate from the change cutoff: with a 60-minute cutoff and a 120-minute window, a student can still cancel online between two hours and one hour before, and it's recorded as late. The cancel page warns them first. The student gets a late-cancellation email instead of the usual one. The tutor's email doesn't change. Staff canceling inside the window check "The student asked to cancel" when that's why; otherwise nothing is recorded, so a center never charges a student for its own cancellations. That box appears only for an appointment with one student, since one late cancellation can't belong to a whole group.

**Waivers keep the record and drop the count.** Front desk staff and directors waive a late cancellation or no-show with a reason. It stays on the student's record, and the export shows it with the reason, but it no longer counts toward a block. A waiver doesn't lift a block already in effect; that stays a director's decision. Like an exemption's note, the reason stays out of the audit log, which records who waived what and when.

**Blocks pause online booking, not the student.** A center sets limits that count every schedule; a schedule can set its own that count only that schedule and pause only that schedule. Each limit counts unwaived no-shows, or late cancellations, within the term of the appointment being counted, or from a date the director chooses. Counting is by the appointment's start, like the booking limits. When a no-show is confirmed or a late cancellation recorded, the check runs in the same transaction after the student's membership is locked, so two at once can't set two blocks or miss one (a test races two). A block ends after the last day of that term unless a director lifts it sooner. Lifting doesn't reset the count, so the next one past the limit sets a new block; to take one out of the count, staff waive it. While blocked, the student can't book for themselves, but their appointments stay and they can still cancel or move them; staff can book for them. The student sees why, when it ends, and whom to write to, on the booking pages and their appointments page. Setting and lifting are audited. Only directors see the list of blocks and lift them, because a block is a judgment about a student, like an exemption.

**Reminders are counted in real time and follow the appointment.** A schedule sets up to three reminders, from 30 minutes to 48 hours ahead; new schedules start with 24 hours and 1 hour. A reminder is due at the start minus its lead time in real hours, like notice and cutoff, so a day-before reminder across a daylight-saving change comes 24 hours ahead, which the clock reads as 23 or 25. The due time comes from the appointment's current start, so a moved appointment's reminders follow it, and the start time is part of what makes each reminder unique, so a moved appointment gets new ones. Canceled, tentative and placeholder appointments get none. A reminder that fell due before the appointment was booked, moved or confirmed is skipped, because the confirmation email just went out; so is a student's that fell due before they joined a group.

**Each reminder is sent once, enforced by the database.** The scheduler writes a `Reminder` row (appointment, recipient, lead time, start) and queues the email in the same transaction. A unique constraint on those four columns means a second scheduler, or a sweep run twice, inserts nothing and sends nothing; a test races two. The reminder also locks the appointment row, so a cancellation or move committing at that moment is seen. Reminders skip anything more than 15 minutes past due, so a scheduler that was down catches up on what is still close to on time and never sends a stack of stale ones. The shortest gap between two reminder choices is 30 minutes, so one sweep never sends two reminders for one appointment.

**Students opt out of reminders; tutors opt in.** The settings are on the account (Account, Email reminders), not per center, and every reminder links to them. A student's reminder names their tutor, the time and the place. A tutor's names no students; they sign in to see who it is. Reminders carry no calendar file, since the booking email already added the appointment.

**The scheduler is its own process.** `manage.py scheduler` sweeps at the start of every minute: reminders first, then possible no-shows. It runs as a fourth Compose service beside web, worker and db. The task worker runs jobs it is handed, and something still has to decide each minute which jobs are due; cron inside a container would need a second process manager. After each sweep that finishes without an error, the scheduler writes the time to a heartbeat file, and `python -m sympraxi.healthcheck scheduler` is healthy only if that time is under three minutes old. A scheduler that can't reach the database writes nothing and turns unhealthy, not only one whose process died. `upgrade.sh` stops it while migrations run and `restore.sh` while the database is replaced. Links in reminders need `SYMPRAXI_BASE_URL`; the production stack fills it in from `SYMPRAXI_DOMAIN`, and the command refuses to start without it.

**The row-lock order gains a level.** Memberships, then tutor assignments, then the appointment, then its participants. Marking attendance and canceling (when a student asked) now lock the students' memberships first, because either can set a block.

## Phase 1: session records (October 2026)

**One record per student, in groups too.** A session record is about one student. A group appointment gets a separate record for each student in it, so no student can see what was written about another. To save typing, the form for a group can start drafts for the rest of the group with the same summary, next steps, topics, length and resources. Staff notes and the writing project aren't copied, because notes are about one student and each student has their own projects. Every copy stays a draft until the tutor reads and finalizes it, and each student gets their own email. Students who left the group get no copy. The alternative, one shared record per group, would have meant either hiding the summary from students or showing each of them what was written about the others.

**Final records are locked in the database.** As with the audit log, a trigger (records migration 0002) does the locking. Once a record is final, an UPDATE of it, or any change to its topic tags or attached resources, is refused unless the same transaction has opened an amendment for that record. `services.amend()` writes the `Amendment` (author, time, reason, and each changed field before and after), sets `sympraxi.amendment` to its ID for the rest of the transaction, makes the change, and clears the setting. Even inside an amendment, the student, tutor, appointment and finalization can't change. Final records can't be deleted, and amendments are append-only. A future retention purge will set `sympraxi.records_purge`. Anyone with a database shell can set either value, so the trigger stops silent edits made through Sympraxi's code or the admin. Like the audit log's trigger, it doesn't stop the database owner.

**What finalizing needs.** A summary for the student, at least one concern tag, and a session that has started. Stages and tutor actions are optional. When a tutor finalizes an incomplete record from the form, it's saved as a draft and the page says what's missing, so nothing typed is lost.

**Who reads records.** Tutors read the records of students they have an appointment with in that center: past or upcoming, but not canceled, and not a group the student left. Upcoming appointments count so the pre-session brief works. Tutors also read the records they wrote. A record for a meeting that wasn't booked doesn't open the student's other records to its tutor; otherwise any tutor could reach a student's history by starting a draft about them. Tutors edit and amend only their own records. Directors read, write and amend every record in their center, and only directors export. Front desk staff see whether a record is final, a draft or not written (on the appointment page, the day sheet and the records-to-write list), never what it says. Site administrators get none of these actions. A student sees only their own final records, and only the parts written for them. As with appointments, someone else's record is a 404 for people who can see some records, so record IDs can't be probed. The build plan lets directors widen or narrow tutors' access per center; that setting isn't built yet.

**Pages about a student use the student's membership.** The history, brief and export are about one student in one center, and the student's `Membership` holds both. Their URLs use the membership's UUID, so no name or student ID appears in a URL, and the policy gets one object that knows both the student and the center.

**Every staff read is audited.** Opening a record or its form, opening a student's history, and each pre-session brief shown on an appointment page write `records.record_viewed` or `records.history_viewed`, with the student's ID and which page it was. Saving, finalizing, amending (with the names of the fields changed), exporting and changing the settings are audited too. No event holds record text. Students reading their own records aren't logged, since that isn't a disclosure.

**Students see summaries, next steps and resources.** `Center.students_see_staff_notes`, off by default, adds the staff notes to the student's My writing page. Either way, the record form tells tutors that students can ask to see everything written there (34 CFR 99.10), and My writing tells students they can ask for a copy. Topic tags aren't shown to students; they're in the export. When a final record is amended, My writing shows the date it was updated but not the earlier text.

**The summary email says it's ready and links to it.** It names the tutor, the center and the date. `Center.summary_email_includes_text`, off by default, adds the summary and next steps. Staff notes never go in an email. The email goes through the queued mailer after the transaction commits, so a rolled-back finalize sends nothing. Amendments don't send another email.

**The FERPA export.** A director can export one student's complete record in their center, as JSON or as a page to print or save as PDF: account details, every appointment (canceled ones and groups the student left included), every session record (drafts included) with staff notes and each amendment's before and after, attached resources with the tutor's notes and each resource's license, and writing projects. Other students are left out (99.12): a group appointment shows how many students it had, never who. Each export is audited. The disclosure log (99.32) comes in a later slice.

**The catalog is loaded by a command.** `manage.py load_catalog` reads `catalog/vocabulary.yaml` and `catalog/resources/*.yaml` and matches on topic slugs and resource IDs, so a second run changes nothing and an edited entry is updated in place. Nothing is deleted. A resource that leaves the catalog is marked inactive, because records may still point to it, and a topic missing from the file is reported. One bad entry stops the whole load. Each resource keeps its license, publisher and (for OpenStax) the CC BY source it can be copied from. The catalog changes between releases without any change to the schema, so `install.sh` and `upgrade.sh` run the command every time, and so does `seed_demo`. The Docker image now includes `catalog/`. It needs PyYAML, the one new dependency.

**Topics and resources live in the records app for now.** The build plan's layout gives resources an app of their own. Today every use of a resource goes through a session record, so the two models share `sympraxi/records`; moving them out later is a matter of two models and their loader.

**Centers add links, not files.** Directors add their center's own resources: a title, an https address, their own summary, publisher, license and topics. Those links are offered only in that center and come first when a suggestion ties. File uploads wait for the upload hardening planned for Phase 2.

**Suggestions follow the concerns.** A resource scores 3 for each concern it shares with the record. One that shares none scores 2 if it has a more specific term under one of the record's concerns (record: `citation`, resource: `citation:apa`), or else 1 if it has a related term (the parent, or another term under the same parent). Related terms don't add to a shared one, so a page tagged both APA and MLA doesn't outrank an APA page on an APA record. Each matching stage or action adds 1, but a resource needs a concern point to be suggested at all, because nearly every resource fits "drafting" or "revising." The top eight show.

**The record form works without JavaScript.** Attach and Search are submit buttons in the form. Without htmx they post the form back and redraw it, unsaved and without error messages. With htmx, checking a topic refreshes the suggestions, the search updates as the tutor types, and Attach adds the resource in place; each request sends only the fields it needs (`hx-params`), so the notes never travel with a search. A status line announces what changed. Resource links on the form open in a new tab and say so, so following one doesn't lose an unsaved record. Concern terms are grouped under their parents in `<details>` elements, which work by keyboard with no script.

**Writing projects are basic.** Tutors start a project from the record form, and the tutor or a director can edit it or mark it finished. Students can't add or edit their own projects yet.

**Records stay out of the Django admin.** The admin is for site administrators, who have no access to records, so none of the records models are registered there.

## Phase 1: single sign-on (October 2026)

**One Entra tenant and one Google domain per installation, set in the environment.** The build plan had each center entering its own tenant's settings. An installation serves one college, though, and a college has one tenant or one Workspace domain. Reading providers from `SYMPRAXI_ENTRA_*` and `SYMPRAXI_GOOGLE_*` variables keeps client secrets out of the database and its backups. allauth's admin screens for apps and tokens are removed, and an app saved in the database is ignored. A provider with only some of its variables set stops the site from starting, rather than quietly leaving SSO off.

**allauth's generic OpenID Connect provider for both.** allauth's Microsoft provider reads the Graph profile, whose `mail` field isn't verified and which carries no tenant or `amr` claim. The generic provider reads the ID token, which has everything the checks need. Userinfo isn't fetched, PKCE is on, and tokens aren't stored. The sign-in URLs are `/accounts/oidc/entra/...` and `/accounts/oidc/google/...`.

**The tenant is pinned three ways.** The discovery document is the tenant's own (`/{tenant}/v2.0`), so allauth rejects ID tokens from any other issuer. `common`, `organizations`, `consumers` and anything that isn't a GUID are refused at startup. `sympraxi/accounts/sso.py` also checks `tid` and `iss` on every sign-in.

**Only addresses the provider vouches for.** Entra's `email` claim is unverified and editable, the hole behind the 2023 "nOAuth" account takeovers. Sympraxi requires Entra's `xms_edov` claim (the address is in a domain the tenant verified), and Google's `email_verified` plus a matching `hd`. When `SYMPRAXI_SIGNUP_EMAIL_DOMAINS` is set, the address must be in it. The checks run on every sign-in, so an account that stops passing them can't keep using SSO.

**Linking by address, with two limits.** A sign-in that passes the checks is linked to an existing account with the same address. After that, sign-ins match the provider's subject ID, so a later change of address doesn't break the link. An account already linked to a different identity at the same provider is never linked again by address: colleges reuse addresses, and a new holder must not inherit the old holder's records. allauth removes the password of a matched account whose address was never verified, so whoever registered it first loses access. Links, unlinks and refused sign-ins are audited (`auth.sso_linked`, `auth.sso_unlinked`, `auth.login_failed` with a reason).

**SSO doesn't skip the staff 2FA rule unless the token proves MFA.** By default allauth's two-factor step runs after SSO like after a password, and staff without a second factor are sent to set one up. `SYMPRAXI_TRUST_IDP_MFA` accepts the provider's MFA only when the ID token's `amr` claim contains `mfa`. Nothing else counts, including Google's `hwk` and `swk`, whose meaning as multi-factor Google doesn't define. Entra sends `amr` once it is added as an optional claim. Google began sending `amr` in June 2026, on request, and only to apps it has verified, so an Internal Workspace app may never receive it; those installations keep Sympraxi's own 2FA.

**SSO-only is enforced at sign-in, not in the template.** `SYMPRAXI_SSO_ONLY` hides the password form, passkey button and sign-up link. `AccountAdapter.pre_login()` refuses every non-SSO sign-in except a site administrator's, from any page. Site administrators keep a break-glass password page at `/accounts/login/local/`, with two-factor login as usual. Without a provider, the setting is refused at startup and ignored at runtime, so it can't lock everyone out.

**SSO buttons are forms, and form-action allows the provider.** Starting sign-in takes a POST, so a link on another site can't start one. Browsers apply the Content Security Policy's `form-action` to the redirect that follows a form, so the policy adds `https://login.microsoftonline.com` or `https://accounts.google.com`, only for providers that are set up. A browser test presses each button from the keyboard and checks that the browser then requests the provider's page, which it does only if the policy allows the redirect. The test browser resolves no host but localhost, so that request, like any other a page might make, never leaves the machine. Playwright can't intercept a request made while following a redirect, so blocking name lookups is the only dependable guard.

**Plain text buttons, no logos.** "Sign in with Microsoft" and "Sign in with Google" use the standard USWDS button. Vendor logos would put trademarked artwork in the repository for no gain in clarity.

## Phase 1: scheduling core (October 2026)

**No double-booking, enforced by PostgreSQL.** An exclusion constraint (`scheduling_appointment_no_tutor_overlap`) rejects two active appointments for the same tutor whose times overlap, on any schedule in any center. The `=` on the tutor column needs the `btree_gist` extension, which the first scheduling migration creates. It is a trusted extension, so the database owner can create it without superuser rights. The booking service checks first so people get a readable message; the constraint catches the race that check can't.

**The break after an appointment is stored on the row.** Each appointment keeps `blocked_until`, its end plus the schedule's buffer at booking time, and the constraint covers `[starts_at, blocked_until)`. The constraint can't add the buffer itself: adding an interval to a `timestamptz` isn't an immutable expression, so PostgreSQL won't index it. Changing a schedule's buffer later leaves existing appointments as they are.

**Row locks in a fixed order.** Booking, changing and canceling each run in one transaction and lock rows in the same order: the students' memberships (their limits and their own calendars), then the tutor assignments by primary key (caps and group seats), then the appointment. Two students racing for the last seat in a group, or one student booking twice at once, can't both get through. The locks use `select_for_update(of=("self",))`, so joined rows such as the center stay unlocked and bookings across a center don't queue behind each other. A booking or a move locks every assignment its tutor has, on every schedule, not only the one being booked. With only that one locked, two students booking one tutor on two schedules at the same moment could both insert, and PostgreSQL stopped one with a deadlock while checking the exclusion constraint; that student got a server error. Now the second booking waits for the first to commit, sees it and is refused. Bookings for one tutor queue even when their times don't overlap, but each holds the locks only for its own short transaction. The constraint stays as the backstop: if it, a deadlock or a serialization failure still stops a booking or a move, the person is told the time was just taken.

**Wall-clock times on daylight-saving nights.** Weekly hours and opening hours are wall-clock times in the center's zone. A wall-clock time stands for the first instant at which the clock reads that time or later. On the night clocks go back, 1:30 means the first 1:30; on the night they go forward, 2:30 means 3:00. Slots step in real time from the opening instant, so the repeated hour gets its own slots and every appointment lasts as long as it says. A weekly series keeps its wall-clock time each week. Notice, horizon and cutoff are measured in real time.

**Limits count by the appointment's start, in local time.** A day is a local calendar day (23 or 25 hours on switch days), a week runs Monday to Sunday, and a term is the schedule's term dates. Canceled appointments and placeholders don't count. When "count appointments on every schedule" is checked, a schedule's limits count the student's appointments on all of the center's schedules; each schedule's own limits apply when booking on it.

**Exemptions cover appointments, not booking days.** An exemption lifts a student's count and minute limits for appointments on or before its last date, so the last day of an exemption can't be used to book a month ahead. Notice, horizon and cutoff still apply. Every exemption has an end date. Adding or ending one is audited, without the note.

**Staff book under different rules.** Front desk and directors aren't held to minimum notice, the horizon or the change cutoff, so they can enter walk-ins and fix mistakes. They are held to student limits and tutor caps unless they check "book even if it goes over," which the audit log records. Nobody can book outside a tutor's hours or over another appointment.

**Students move their own one-to-one appointments; staff move groups.** Until the change cutoff, a student who is the only person in an appointment can move it to another open time on the same schedule. The move keeps the appointment's row and calendar UID, raises its `SEQUENCE`, and re-checks every booking rule while leaving the appointment itself out of the counts. Moving to another schedule means canceling and booking again. Only staff move group appointments, because a move changes the time for everyone in the group. Moving has its own policy action, `scheduling.change_appointment`, granted like canceling.

**Grants that reach only a person's own records.** Students can view, cancel and move only the appointments they are in, and tutors can view only the appointments they teach. These limits are `OBJECT_RULES` in `policy.py`, beside the role grants, and `tests/test_policy_matrix.py` lists them by hand. Someone else's appointment returns 404, as it does for an outsider, so appointment IDs can't be probed. Students never see other students: open times carry no names, and only staff and the tutor see who is in a group.

**Site administrators get no scheduling actions.** Appointments are student records, so the Phase 0 rule holds: they are reached through a center role, not through `is_superuser`.

**Two statuses and three flags.** An appointment is scheduled or canceled. Walk-in, placeholder and tentative are flags on a scheduled appointment, and a tentative booking holds its time until staff confirm or cancel it. The constraint ignores only canceled appointments, so a status added later that still holds the time (completed, missed) is covered without a migration.

**Extra hours stay inside opening hours.** A tutor's extra hours can add time on a day they don't usually work, but never past the schedule's opening hours. A center that stays open late for finals changes the schedule's hours.

**Appointment emails say as little as possible.** A student's email names their tutor, the time and the place. A tutor's email says a student booked and links to the appointment, without the student's name. Mail goes out after the transaction commits, so a booking that is rolled back sends nothing. `SYMPRAXI_BASE_URL` sets the links in mail sent outside a page request, such as the reminders still to come.

**Calendar files are written by hand.** An appointment needs about a dozen iCalendar properties, and the rules that matter (escaping, CRLF line endings, folding at 75 octets) fit in one short module, so there is no `icalendar` dependency. Each appointment keeps one UID. Bookings and changes go out as `METHOD:PUBLISH` with a rising `SEQUENCE`, cancellations as `METHOD:CANCEL`. Calendar apps vary in how they handle these, so the email text is the record. The queued mailer now carries attachments, as text or as base64 for binary files.

**Property-based tests with Hypothesis.** A state machine books, cancels and moves appointments at random on two schedules that share a tutor, across a daylight-saving week, and checks the tables after every step: no tutor or student is double-booked and no limit, cap or group size is exceeded. The checks are computed independently of the booking code. The default run plays the same sequences every time, so CI is repeatable, and it fails if those sequences never book, cancel, move or reach a limit, so it can't pass by being refused everything. `HYPOTHESIS_PROFILE=thorough` plays about twelve times as many, chosen at random. A separate test races real threads in committed transactions.

**PostgreSQL 18 for local tests on Windows.** The pgserver package's PostgreSQL has no contrib extensions, so it can't create `btree_gist`. The local runs for this phase used the EDB PostgreSQL 18.6 binaries zip, started from a scratch folder on its own port. The `postgres` Docker images and the Debian and Ubuntu packages include `btree_gist`.

## Phase 0 (October 2026)

**Django 6.1 now, Django 6.2 LTS before the pilot.** The build plan first said "5.2 LTS now." Moving from 5.2 to 6.2 crosses a major version. Moving from 6.1 to 6.2 is a single feature release, and 6.2 LTS ships in April 2027, before the summer pilot. Django 6.x also brings two things Sympraxi would otherwise need packages for: a built-in Content Security Policy with nonces, and the Tasks framework. Renovate is set not to jump to 6.2 automatically.

**Background jobs: Django's Tasks framework with the `django-tasks-db` backend.** Jobs are stored in PostgreSQL and run by `manage.py db_worker`, so a small install needs no Redis or Valkey. The jobs are written against `django.tasks`, so a different backend can be swapped in later without code changes.

**Email through `MAILERS` (new in Django 6.1).** The `default` mailer hands messages to the task worker. The `delivery` mailer talks to the campus SMTP relay. A slow relay never holds up a page.

**Shared cache in the database.** Login rate limiting needs a cache that all Gunicorn workers share. Django's database cache does that without another service. Set `CACHE_URL` to a Valkey URL if a large install needs it.

**Design system: U.S. Web Design System 3.14.** It is built and tested for accessibility, and its code is in the public domain (CC0); its fonts are under the SIL Open Font License and its icons under Apache 2.0. We use its components and styles but not the U.S. government banner or anything that implies a government site. GOV.UK Frontend was the alternative; its typeface and crown are restricted to UK government services.

**Assets are copied from npm packages, not loaded from a CDN.** A self-hosted install makes no third-party requests, which keeps the Content Security Policy at `'self'` and keeps students' browsing private.

**Server-rendered pages with htmx.** Less JavaScript, fewer accessibility traps, a smaller attack surface. htmx is configured with `allowEval` and `allowScriptTags` off and without its inline indicator styles, so it works under the strict CSP.

**Permissions in one module.** `sympraxi/core/policy.py` holds every rule. A hand-written test matrix and a URL inventory test keep it honest. Outsiders get 404 rather than 403, so URLs can't be used to discover which centers or records exist.

**Site administrators are not a back door to records.** Superusers get operational actions only (`SITE_ADMIN_GRANTS`). Actions that read student records will be granted by center role, never to site admins by default.

**The audit log is append-only in the database.** A PostgreSQL trigger rejects `UPDATE` and `DELETE` on `privacy_auditevent`. A future retention purge will set `SET LOCAL sympraxi.audit_purge = 'on'` for its own transaction. The actor and center are stored as IDs without foreign-key constraints, plus a text label, so deleting a person or center never rewrites history.

**Two-factor login required for every staff role.** Tutors, front desk, directors and site admins are sent to the 2FA setup page until they add an authenticator app or a passkey. Text-message codes are not offered. The Django admin uses the same sign-in, so the same rule applies there.

**Python 3.12 in Docker and CI**, matching development machines. Security support for 3.12 runs to October 2028.

**PostgreSQL 18 in production and CI.** The local test run on Windows used a portable PostgreSQL 16 because Docker wasn't available. Nothing in Phase 0 depends on 18.
